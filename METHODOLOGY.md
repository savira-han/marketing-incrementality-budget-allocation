# Methodology

## 01 - Analytical Framework

The analysis follows four connected measurement layers:

**Funnel → Attribution → Incrementality → Economics**

Each layer answers a different question.

| Layer          | Question                                                                |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Funnel         | What happens from marketing exposure to purchase?                       |
| Attribution    | Which marketing interactions receive credit for observed purchases?     |
| Incrementality | Which outcomes are caused by additional marketing investment?           |
| Economics      | How much contribution margin is generated by that incremental activity? |

These layers are intentionally separated because attributed performance and incremental performance are not equivalent.

---

## 02 - Marketing Performance

Marketing performance is measured across Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM.

Organic activity is included as a measurement baseline but is not eligible for the additional budget.

The starting paid-channel allocation is:

* Google: 40%
* Meta: 30%
* TikTok: 20%
* CRM: 10%

This allocation is the starting condition for the analysis, not the final recommendation.

Core marketing metrics include:

* Spend
* Impressions
* Clicks
* Purchases
* Revenue
* Contribution margin

---

## 03 - Multi-Touch Attribution

The primary attribution approach is **multi-touch attribution**.

Customer marketing journeys are constructed from marketing touchpoints and transactions.

A touchpoint is considered eligible for attribution when it occurs within **14 days before a purchase**.

Last-touch attribution is retained as a comparison baseline, but it is not the primary attribution methodology.

Attribution is used to understand how observed purchases are distributed across customer journeys and channels.

It is **not treated as causal evidence**.

---

## 04 - Geographic Incrementality Experiment

Incrementality is measured through a geographic experiment.

The experiment uses:

* 20 geographic areas
* 10 treatment areas
* 10 control areas
* 28-day experiment period

Areas are assigned using **stratified randomization based on pre-experiment characteristics**.

Treatment areas receive a **25% increase in total marketing spend**, while control areas remain at baseline.

The treatment is designed to preserve the existing 4:3:2:1 channel mix while increasing total marketing investment.

The experiment therefore estimates the aggregate incremental effect of increasing total marketing investment.

It does **not** independently identify the causal effect of an individual channel.

---

## 05 - Difference-in-Differences

The primary causal estimator is **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)**.

The basic comparison is:

**Change in treatment areas - Change in control areas**

The primary causal outcome is contribution margin.

Purchases and revenue are supporting outcomes.

The validity of the estimate depends on the treatment and control areas having sufficiently similar pre-experiment trends.

The analysis therefore evaluates:

* Baseline balance
* Pre-experiment trends
* Marketing exposure
* Treatment contamination
* Seasonality
* Area-level shocks
* Statistical uncertainty

---

## 06 - Customer Economics

Customer economics connects observed customer behavior to economic value.

Customer-level analysis may include:

* Purchase frequency
* Revenue
* Contribution margin
* Customer value
* Customer segments based on observed behavior

Acquisition date is derived from the customer's first observed transaction rather than stored as a source attribute.

Customer segmentation is derived from observed behavior rather than generated as a source field.

---

## 07 - Channel Response and Marginal Economics

Historical marketing activity and business outcomes are used to estimate how channel performance changes as spend increases.

The analysis focuses on **marginal economics**, rather than average historical return alone.

This distinction is important because a channel that performs well at its current spending level may not generate the same return from additional investment.

Channel response analysis is combined with incrementality evidence to inform the interpretation of incremental marketing effects.

The aggregate geographic experiment provides causal evidence for total marketing investment, while channel response modeling provides information about how individual channels respond to changes in spend.

Individual channel causal effects are not inferred directly from the aggregate experiment.

---

## 08 - Budget Allocation

The final decision problem is:

> Given an additional $2M marketing budget for the next quarter, how should the budget be allocated across Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM to maximize expected incremental contribution margin?

The allocation is evaluated using estimated marginal economics and response curves, subject to the project's defined business constraints.

The starting 4:3:2:1 allocation is treated as the baseline scenario rather than the optimization target.

---

## 09 - Economic Definition

Contribution margin is defined as:

**Contribution Margin = Revenue - Subsidy - Discount**

Subsidy and discount represent transaction-level variable costs.

Marketing spend is kept separate from transaction variable costs and is used in marketing efficiency and budget allocation analysis.

This allows the analysis to distinguish:

* Revenue generated
* Contribution margin generated
* Marketing investment required
* Incremental contribution generated by additional investment

---

## 10 - Simulation Validation

The project uses synthetic data generated from a coherent business process.

Known underlying relationships are stored separately as hidden simulation truth.

These truth tables are not used as inputs to the business analysis.

They are used only after analysis to evaluate whether the analytical methods can reasonably recover the underlying effects.

This provides a validation layer for:

* Incrementality estimates
* Customer value estimates
* Area treatment effects
* Channel response curves

Estimated results are not expected to exactly match simulation truth because the synthetic business contains randomness, heterogeneity, and realistic variation.
