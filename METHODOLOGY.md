# Methodology

## 01 - Analytical Framework

The analysis follows four connected measurement layers:

**Funnel → Attribution → Incrementality → Economics**

Each layer answers a different question.

| Layer          | Question                                                                |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Funnel         | What happens from marketing exposure to purchase?                       |
| Attribution    | Which marketing interactions receive credit for observed purchases?     |
| Incrementality | Which outcomes are caused by additional marketing investment?           |
| Economics      | How much contribution margin is generated by that incremental activity? |

These layers are intentionally separated because attributed performance and incremental performance are not equivalent.

---

## 02 - Marketing Performance

Marketing performance is measured across Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM.

Organic activity is included as a measurement baseline but is not eligible for the additional budget.

The starting paid-channel allocation is:

* Google: 40%
* Meta: 30%
* TikTok: 20%
* CRM: 10%

This allocation is the starting condition for the analysis, not the final recommendation.

Core marketing metrics include:

* Spend
* Impressions
* Clicks
* Purchases
* Revenue
* Contribution margin

---

## 03 - Multi-Touch Attribution

The primary attribution approach is **multi-touch attribution**.

Customer marketing journeys are constructed from marketing touchpoints and transactions.

A touchpoint is considered eligible for attribution when it occurs within **14 days before a purchase**.

Last-touch attribution is retained as a comparison baseline, but it is not the primary attribution methodology.

Attribution is used to understand how observed purchases are distributed across customer journeys and channels.

It is **not treated as causal evidence**.

---

## 04 - Geographic Incrementality Experiment

Incrementality is measured through a geographic experiment.

The experiment uses:

* 20 geographic areas
* 10 treatment areas
* 10 control areas
* 28-day experiment period

Areas are assigned using **stratified randomization based on pre-experiment characteristics**.

Treatment areas receive a **25% increase in total marketing spend**, while control areas remain at baseline.

The treatment is designed to preserve the existing 4:3:2:1 channel mix while increasing total marketing investment.

The experiment therefore estimates the aggregate incremental effect of increasing total marketing investment.

It does **not** independently identify the causal effect of an individual channel.

---

## 05 - Difference-in-Differences

The primary causal estimator is **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)**.

The basic comparison is:

**Change in treatment areas - Change in control areas**

The primary causal outcome is contribution margin.

Purchases and revenue are supporting outcomes.

The validity of the estimate depends on the treatment and control areas having sufficiently similar pre-experiment trends.

The analysis therefore evaluates:

* Baseline balance
* Pre-experiment trends
* Marketing exposure
* Treatment contamination
* Seasonality
* Area-level shocks
* Statistical uncertainty

---

## 06 - Customer Economics

Customer economics connects observed customer behavior to economic value.

Customer-level analysis may include:

* Purchase frequency
* Revenue
* Contribution margin
* Customer value
* Customer segments based on observed behavior

Acquisition date is derived from the customer's first observed transaction rather than stored as a source attribute.

Customer segmentation is derived from observed behavior rather than generated as a source field.

### Transaction Economics

All transaction economics are denominated in **USD**.

Contribution margin is calculated from transaction-level revenue and variable promotional costs:

**Contribution Margin = Revenue - Subsidy - Discount**

Revenue, subsidy, and discount are stored at the transaction level. Contribution margin is derived during validation and analysis rather than generated as a raw transaction field.

Marketing spend remains separate from transaction-level variable costs.

This allows the analysis to distinguish between:

* Transaction contribution margin
* Marketing investment
* Incremental contribution generated by marketing investment

### Customer and Area Economic Heterogeneity

Customer and area records contain persistent economic tendencies that influence transaction outcomes.

These include:

* Customer AOV tendency
* Area AOV tendency
* Customer price sensitivity
* Customer repeat purchase tendency
* Customer purchase propensity

AOV is generated from customer and area tendencies combined with transaction-level variation.

The customer and area tendencies create persistent differences in realized booking value while preserving stochastic variation between individual transactions.

The economic scale is designed around realistic USD booking values rather than applying an IDR-based transaction distribution to a USD marketing environment.

### Promotional Costs

Discount and subsidy are modeled as transaction-level variable costs.

Customer price sensitivity influences the level of discount and subsidy received.

Promotional costs are bounded so that the generated business does not produce structurally unrealistic transaction economics.

Contribution margin is therefore allowed to vary across customers and transactions while remaining positive under the current simulation design.

### Repeat Purchasing

Customer purchasing is modeled as a state-based process rather than assigning a fixed number of purchases to each customer.

A customer can move through multiple purchase states:

**No purchase → First purchase → Repeat purchase → Further repeat purchase**

Repeat purchase behavior is influenced by the customer's persistent repeat purchase tendency.

Higher repeat purchase tendency increases the likelihood of continued purchasing and shortens the expected waiting period between purchases.

Repeat purchase timing is therefore generated as a behavioral process rather than a fixed interval.

Customer value emerges from the resulting transaction history rather than being directly assigned.

This allows customers with different purchase frequencies to naturally produce different levels of cumulative revenue and contribution margin.

---

## 07 - Channel Response and Marginal Economics

Historical marketing activity and business outcomes are used to estimate how channel performance changes as spend increases.

The analysis focuses on **marginal economics**, rather than average historical return alone.

This distinction is important because a channel that performs well at its current spending level may not generate the same return from additional investment.

Channel response analysis is combined with incrementality evidence to inform the interpretation of incremental marketing effects.

The aggregate geographic experiment provides causal evidence for total marketing investment, while channel response modeling provides information about how individual channels respond to changes in spend.

Individual channel causal effects are not inferred directly from the aggregate experiment.

---

## 08 - Budget Allocation

The final decision problem is:

> Given an additional $2M marketing budget for the next quarter, how should the budget be allocated across Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM to maximize expected incremental contribution margin?

The allocation is evaluated using estimated marginal economics and response curves, subject to the project's defined business constraints.

The starting 4:3:2:1 allocation is treated as the baseline scenario rather than the optimization target.

---

## 09 - Economic Definition

Contribution margin is defined as:

**Contribution Margin = Revenue - Subsidy - Discount**

Subsidy and discount represent transaction-level variable costs.

Marketing spend is kept separate from transaction variable costs and is used in marketing efficiency and budget allocation analysis.

This allows the analysis to distinguish:

* Revenue generated
* Contribution margin generated
* Marketing investment required
* Incremental contribution generated by additional investment

Customer value is derived from observed transaction history rather than directly assigned.

A customer's cumulative economic value therefore depends on:

* Number of completed purchases
* Transaction revenue
* Transaction-level discount
* Transaction-level subsidy
* Resulting contribution margin

This preserves the distinction between first-purchase economics and customer-level value.

---

## 10 - Simulation Validation

The project uses synthetic data generated from a coherent business process.

Known underlying relationships are stored separately as hidden simulation truth.

These truth tables are not used as inputs to the business analysis.

They are used only after analysis to evaluate whether the analytical methods can reasonably recover the underlying effects.

This provides a validation layer for:

* Incrementality estimates
* Customer value estimates
* Area treatment effects
* Channel response curves

Estimated results are not expected to exactly match simulation truth because the synthetic business contains randomness, heterogeneity, and realistic variation.

---

## 11 - Synthetic Data Quality and Integration Standard

The synthetic dataset is designed as one integrated business system rather than a collection of independently generated tables.

Each table has a defined grain, primary key, foreign-key relationships, business purpose, and analytical role. Generation logic follows these dependencies so that customer behavior, marketing activity, transactions, revenue, and contribution margin remain connected throughout the dataset.

### Generation Principles

Synthetic data generation follows the dependency structure of the business rather than generating each table independently.

The main dependency flow is:

**Areas → Marketing Performance → Customers → Customer Behavior → Transactions → Marketing Touchpoints → Attribution and Analysis**

Persistent customer and area characteristics are generated before downstream behavioral outcomes so that the same characteristics can influence multiple parts of the business.

This creates coherent relationships across the dataset instead of relying on independent random distributions.

Customer behavior is modeled using persistent hidden characteristics including:

* Purchase propensity
* AOV tendency
* Repeat purchase tendency
* Price sensitivity
* Channel responsiveness

Area-level characteristics influence customer acquisition and economic behavior, while marketing activity influences customer exposure and purchase opportunities.

Transactions are then generated from the interaction between customer characteristics, area characteristics, marketing activity, seasonality, and stochastic variation.

### Transaction Generation

Transactions contain:

* Transaction ID
* Customer ID
* Transaction date
* Revenue
* Subsidy
* Discount
* Cancellation status

Contribution margin is derived from these fields rather than stored independently.

Completed transactions contribute revenue and variable costs to customer economics.

Cancelled transactions do not contribute revenue, subsidy, discount, or contribution margin.

### Validation Layers

Before the dataset is considered analysis-ready, it must pass five validation layers:

1. **Structural integrity**

   * Required tables and columns exist
   * Primary keys are unique
   * Required fields are populated
   * Data types and value ranges are valid

2. **Referential integrity**

   * Foreign keys resolve to valid parent records
   * Customers, areas, campaigns, marketing touches, and transactions can be connected across tables
   * No orphan records exist

3. **Business consistency**

   * Marketing metrics reconcile with their underlying components
   * Revenue and contribution margin calculations reconcile
   * Customer and transaction relationships follow the defined business rules
   * Dates remain within the intended observation windows
   * Cancelled transactions do not contribute transaction economics

4. **Experiment integrity**

   * Treatment and control assignments follow the experiment design
   * Areas are assigned consistently
   * Pre-period and experiment-period observations are available
   * Outcome data is connected to the assigned experiment units without unintended leakage

5. **Behavioral and analytical validity**

   * Generated distributions and relationships are commercially plausible
   * Customer acquisition, repeat behavior, revenue, and contribution margin exhibit intentional relationships
   * Marketing exposure and customer outcomes are generated from coherent behavioral mechanisms
   * Persistent customer and area characteristics produce the intended directional relationships
   * Hidden simulation truth is consistent with the mechanisms used to generate the observed data

The final dataset is considered analysis-ready only when all validation layers pass.

The objective is not to make the synthetic data look realistic in isolation. The objective is to create a coherent synthetic business in which the relationships between marketing activity, customer behavior, financial outcomes, experimentation, and incrementality are sufficiently consistent to support the project's downstream analytical methods.
