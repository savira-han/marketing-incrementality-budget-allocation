# Assumptions

## 01 - Business Assumptions

### The company is an online travel platform focused on hotel bookings

**Reason:** The project requires a realistic business context for customer journeys, marketing behavior, and economic modeling.

**Impact:** Hotel booking is assumed to involve meaningful consideration and multiple marketing interactions, supporting a 14-day attribution window.

**Status:** Locked

---

### The primary business outcome is Purchase

**Reason:** The core marketing decision concerns generating hotel purchases rather than separating first and repeat purchases in standard campaign reporting.

**Impact:** Purchase is the primary conversion outcome. Repeat-purchase behavior may be used later for customer economics and customer value analysis.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Contribution margin is the primary economic outcome

**Definition:**

`Contribution Margin = Revenue - Subsidy - Discount`

**Reason:** Revenue alone does not represent the economic value generated by marketing.

**Impact:** Budget allocation is evaluated against contribution margin rather than revenue alone.

**Status:** Locked

---

## 02 - Marketing Assumptions

### The starting paid-channel mix is 4:3:2:1

Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM currently represent approximately 40%, 30%, 20%, and 10% of paid marketing investment.

**Reason:** This provides a realistic baseline for the budget allocation scenario.

**Impact:** The mix is used as the starting condition, not as the target allocation.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Organic is measurement-only

Organic activity is included as a baseline for understanding overall demand but cannot receive the additional $2M budget.

**Reason:** The optimization decision concerns controllable paid/CRM investment.

**Impact:** Organic is excluded from the budget allocation decision.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Additional treatment spend preserves the existing channel mix

Treatment areas receive a 25% increase in total marketing spend while maintaining the existing channel proportions.

**Reason:** The geographic experiment is designed to estimate the aggregate effect of increasing marketing investment rather than independently manipulating individual channels.

**Impact:** The experiment identifies an aggregate marketing effect and cannot directly identify channel-specific causal effects.

**Status:** Locked

---

## 03 - Experiment Assumptions

### Geographic areas can be used as experimental units

**Reason:** Marketing investment and business outcomes can be observed at the area level.

**Impact:** Treatment and control areas can be compared over time.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Treatment and control areas are assigned using stratified randomization

**Reason:** Area characteristics can differ substantially, so randomization is performed within meaningful strata rather than relying on purely unrestricted random assignment.

**Impact:** Baseline balance must still be validated after assignment.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Parallel trends is the key DiD assumption

The Difference-in-Differences approach assumes that treatment and control areas would have followed sufficiently similar outcome trends in the absence of the treatment.

**Impact:** Pre-experiment trends must be evaluated before interpreting the causal estimate.

**Status:** Required assumption

---

### The experiment estimates aggregate marketing incrementality

**Reason:** All eligible channels are increased together.

**Impact:** The experiment cannot be used to claim that a specific channel caused the measured incremental effect.

**Status:** Locked

---

## 04 - Attribution Assumptions

### Customer marketing interactions are represented by clicks

**Reason:** Clicks represent meaningful customer interactions while keeping the customer-level journey dataset manageable.

**Impact:** Impressions remain in marketing performance data but are not treated as customer touchpoints for multi-touch attribution.

**Status:** Locked

---

### The primary attribution window is 14 days

**Reason:** Hotel booking decisions can involve consideration and multiple interactions, making a 7-day window potentially restrictive while a 30-day window may include stale interactions.

**Impact:** Touchpoints occurring more than 14 days before a purchase are excluded from the primary attribution calculation.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Attribution is descriptive rather than causal

**Reason:** Receiving attribution credit does not establish that the marketing interaction caused the purchase.

**Impact:** Incrementality is evaluated separately through the geographic experiment.

**Status:** Locked

---

## 05 - Data Assumptions

### Source data represents one coherent synthetic business process

**Reason:** Independent random datasets would not produce realistic relationships between marketing activity, customers, touchpoints, and transactions.

**Impact:** Source tables must be generated from connected underlying business processes.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Area information can be derived from campaign naming

Raw marketing data does not directly contain an area field. Area information is encoded in campaign names and extracted during transformation.

**Reason:** This reflects a realistic situation where marketing datasets may contain campaign identifiers or naming conventions rather than a clean geographic dimension.

**Impact:** Campaign naming consistency becomes an important data-quality consideration.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Customer acquisition date is derived

Acquisition date is defined as the customer's first observed transaction.

**Reason:** It is a derived behavioral attribute rather than a required source field.

**Impact:** Acquisition cohorts are calculated from transaction history.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Customer segments are derived from observed behavior

Customer segments are not generated as source attributes.

**Reason:** Segmentation should reflect measurable customer behavior rather than an arbitrary synthetic label.

**Impact:** Segment definitions are created during analysis.

**Status:** Locked

---

## 06 - Simulation Assumptions

### The synthetic world contains heterogeneous areas and customers

Areas and customers differ in demand, behavior, economics, and marketing response.

**Reason:** Perfectly homogeneous synthetic data would make the analytical problem unrealistic and overly easy.

**Impact:** Analytical estimates will contain natural variation.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Marketing response includes diminishing returns

Additional spend is not assumed to produce a constant return indefinitely.

**Reason:** Budget allocation requires understanding marginal rather than constant channel returns.

**Impact:** Response curves must allow incremental return to change as spend increases.

**Status:** Locked

---

### Hidden simulation truth is not used to produce analytical results

Known underlying effects are stored separately for validation.

**Reason:** The project should test whether the analytical methods can recover underlying relationships rather than directly using those relationships as model inputs.

**Impact:** Simulation truth is accessible only as a validation artifact.

**Status:** Locked

---

### The final optimal allocation is not predetermined

The synthetic data-generating process should not be designed to guarantee a particular channel receives the largest incremental budget.

**Reason:** The portfolio should demonstrate analytical reasoning rather than reverse-engineering a predetermined recommendation.

**Impact:** The final allocation must emerge from the analysis.

**Status:** Locked

---

## 07 - Data Quality Assumptions

The synthetic data may contain realistic imperfections such as:

* Missing campaign identifiers
* Missing UTM information
* Duplicate records
* Customer identity inconsistencies
* Attribution-window differences
* Timezone differences
* Platform and warehouse discrepancies
* Unmatched records
* Experiment contamination

The exact issues and rates will be defined during data generation.

These imperfections should remain realistic and limited enough that the underlying analytical problem remains interpretable.

**Status:** Partially defined
