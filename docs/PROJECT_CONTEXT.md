# Project Context

## 1. Project Overview

**Project:** Marketing Incrementality & Budget Allocation
**Project Type:** Marketing Analytics / Incrementality / Budget Optimization
**Status:** Day 2 - Data Architecture complete
**Data:** Synthetic, controlled simulation
**Primary Business Outcome:** Incremental Contribution Margin

This project develops an end-to-end marketing analytics framework for evaluating marketing effectiveness and determining how an additional marketing budget should be allocated across channels.

The analysis is designed to move from marketing and customer data toward a financially grounded investment decision while distinguishing reported performance from causal impact and average performance from marginal return.

At the current stage, the business and measurement design and the data architecture have been defined. Synthetic data generation and analysis have not yet started.

---

## 2. Business Context

The company is an **online travel platform (OTA) focused on hotel bookings**.

The company currently spends approximately **$10M annually** across:

* Google
* Meta
* TikTok
* CRM
* Organic

The CMO has an additional **$2M** available for investment in the next quarter.

The central business question is:

> Given an additional $2M marketing budget for the next quarter, how should the budget be allocated across Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM to maximize expected incremental contribution margin?

Organic is included for measurement and baseline context but is not eligible for the additional budget.

The final allocation is not predetermined.

## 4. Analytical Objective

The primary objective is to estimate and ultimately optimize:

> **Expected Incremental Contribution Margin**

The project distinguishes between:

* Attributed performance and incremental impact
* Revenue and contribution margin
* Average return and marginal return
* First purchase value and longer-term customer value
* Statistical significance and business significance
* Expected outcomes and uncertainty

The purpose is not simply to identify the channel with the highest historical ROAS. The analysis will focus on how additional investment is expected to change incremental economic value.

## 6. Core Analytical Principles

### 6.1 Attribution ≠ Incrementality

Attribution describes how purchase credit is distributed across marketing touchpoints under an attribution methodology.

Incrementality asks what additional business would have occurred because of the marketing activity.

Attribution is therefore treated as a measurement framework, not direct evidence of causal impact.

### 6.2 Revenue ≠ Contribution Margin

Revenue alone does not represent the economic value generated by marketing.

For this project:

```text
Variable Cost = Subsidy + Discount

Contribution Margin = Revenue - Subsidy - Discount
```

Marketing spend remains separate in the marketing performance data and is not included in transaction-level variable cost.

Contribution margin is the primary financial outcome for the investment decision.

### 6.3 Average Return ≠ Marginal Return

Historical average performance does not necessarily describe the return generated by additional spending.

The project therefore focuses on incremental and marginal economics.

### 6.4 First Purchase Value ≠ Customer Value

A customer's first purchase does not necessarily represent the full economic value of the customer.

Repeat behavior and longer-term customer economics may be evaluated separately from standard campaign conversion reporting.

### 6.5 Statistical Significance ≠ Business Significance

Statistical evidence will be evaluated alongside effect size and economic relevance.

### 6.6 Recommendation ≠ Certainty

The final allocation will be presented with assumptions, uncertainty, sensitivity, and limitations.

## 7. Causal Measurement Design

The project uses a **geographic controlled experiment** to establish aggregate causal evidence.

### Experiment Design

* 20 areas
* 10 treatment areas
* 10 control areas
* 28-day experiment
* Treatment receives a 25% increase in total marketing spend
* Control remains at baseline spend
* Treatment and control are assigned using stratified randomization
* Baseline balance and pre-period trends will be checked before estimating the treatment effect

An **area** is a geographic business unit with independently observable marketing activity and customer outcomes.

The project uses **area** rather than market as the geographic unit.

Areas do not need to be identical. The design relies on stratified randomization followed by baseline-balance and pre-trend diagnostics.

Potential characteristics for stratification and balance checks include:

* Baseline purchases
* Baseline revenue
* Baseline marketing spend
* Area size
* Historical purchase behavior
* Average order value
* Customer mix

The exact variables used for stratification have not yet been finalized.

---

## 8. Treatment Structure

Treatment areas receive a **25% increase in total marketing spend** while preserving the existing 4:3:2:1 channel mix.

For example:

| Channel   |  Baseline | Treatment |
| --------- | --------: | --------: |
| Google    |      $40K |      $50K |
| Meta      |      $30K |    $37.5K |
| TikTok    |      $20K |      $25K |
| CRM       |      $10K |    $12.5K |
| **Total** | **$100K** | **$125K** |

This experiment estimates the aggregate causal effect of increasing total marketing investment.

It does **not** independently identify the causal effect of Google, Meta, TikTok, or CRM because all channels are increased together.

Therefore:

```text
Experiment
    ↓
Aggregate causal evidence
```

while:

```text
Experiment evidence
    +
Attribution
    +
Channel response models
    ↓
Channel-level marginal economics
```

The aggregate experiment effect should not be attributed to an individual channel without sufficient identification.

---

## 9. Difference-in-Differences

The primary causal estimator is **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)**.

Conceptually:

```text
Incremental Effect
=
Change in Treatment
-
Change in Control
```

The primary causal outcome is:

* Contribution margin

Supporting outcomes are:

* Purchases
* Revenue

The key identifying assumption is that treatment and control areas would have followed sufficiently similar trends in the absence of treatment.

Planned diagnostics include:

* Baseline balance
* Pre-treatment trends
* Treatment exposure
* Contamination
* Seasonality
* Area-level shocks
* Statistical uncertainty

No experiment effect has been estimated yet.

## 10. Attribution Design

The primary attribution methodology is **multi-touch attribution**.

Last-touch attribution will be used only as a comparison baseline.

The attribution model will be built as a downstream analytical transformation rather than generated as source data.

The source data will contain actual customer marketing interactions and transactions. Attribution logic will then determine how purchase credit is distributed across eligible touchpoints.

### Touchpoint Definition

A marketing touchpoint represents a **customer click or meaningful marketing interaction**.

Ad impressions are not stored as individual customer-level touchpoints.

Impressions remain in `marketing_performance`.

This keeps the customer journey focused on meaningful interactions and avoids unnecessarily large customer-level datasets.

### Attribution Window

The primary attribution window is **14 days**.

A touchpoint is eligible to receive attribution credit for a purchase if it occurred within 14 days before the purchase.

The 14-day window was selected because the business is an OTA where hotel purchases can involve research, comparison, multiple visits, and interactions across several channels.

A 7-day window was considered too restrictive for the expected consideration journey.

A 30-day window may capture older interactions that have a weaker connection to the eventual purchase.

Other attribution windows may be used later as sensitivity checks, but 14 days is the primary setting.

## 11. Time Architecture

The synthetic business will use one coherent timeline.

### Historical Period

**18 months**

Used to establish:

* Historical area differences
* Baseline demand
* Customer behavior
* Marketing behavior
* Seasonality
* Pre-experiment trends
* Variation in marketing investment

### Experiment Period

**28 days**

Used for the geographic treatment/control experiment and Difference-in-Differences analysis.

### Post-Experiment Observation Period

**90 days**

Used to observe downstream customer behavior and support customer economics.

The 90-day observation period is separate from the 14-day attribution window.

The attribution window determines whether a marketing touchpoint can receive credit for a purchase.

The observation period determines how long downstream customer behavior can be observed after the experiment.

## 12. Data Architecture

The source layer consists of six tables:

```text
areas
experiment_assignment
marketing_performance
customers
marketing_touchpoints
transactions
```

The source tables are designed to represent one coherent business process rather than independent random datasets.

---

## 13. Source Table Definitions

### 13.1 `areas`

**Grain:** one row per area.

| Column      | Purpose               |
| ----------- | --------------------- |
| `area_id`   | Primary identifier    |
| `area_name` | Descriptive area name |
| `area_size` | Optional metadata     |

`area_size` is not used analytically.

Baseline demand, revenue, purchase behavior, and marketing performance are not stored in this table. These characteristics will be derived from historical data.

---

### 13.2 `experiment_assignment`

**Grain:** one row per area per experiment.

| Column             | Purpose                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| `experiment_id`    | Experiment identifier           |
| `area_id`          | Area assigned to the experiment |
| `experiment_group` | Treatment or control            |
| `assignment_date`  | Assignment date                 |

**Key:** `experiment_id + area_id`

Baseline metrics and treatment effects are not stored in this table.

---

### 13.3 `marketing_performance`

**Raw grain:** one campaign × channel × date.

| Column          | Purpose                                   |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `date`          | Marketing activity date                   |
| `channel`       | Google, Meta, TikTok, or CRM              |
| `campaign_id`   | Campaign identifier                       |
| `campaign_name` | Campaign name containing area information |
| `spend`         | Marketing spend                           |
| `impressions`   | Impressions delivered                     |
| `clicks`        | Clicks generated                          |

`area_id` is intentionally not stored in the raw table.

Area is derived from the campaign naming convention.

Example:

```text
BrandSearch_JKT
        ↓
campaign_name parsing
        ↓
area_id = JKT
```

This represents a realistic transformation requirement where geographic information is embedded in campaign naming rather than provided as a clean analytical field.

---

### 13.4 `customers`

**Grain:** one row per customer.

| Column        | Purpose             |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| `customer_id` | Customer identifier |
| `area_id`     | Customer's area     |

`acquisition_date` and `customer_segment` are not stored as source fields.

They will be derived later from observed customer behavior.

---

### 13.5 `marketing_touchpoints`

**Raw grain:** one customer click or marketing interaction.

| Column                 | Purpose                                   |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `touchpoint_id`        | Unique touchpoint identifier              |
| `customer_id`          | Customer associated with the interaction  |
| `touchpoint_timestamp` | Exact interaction timestamp               |
| `channel`              | Marketing channel                         |
| `campaign_id`          | Campaign identifier                       |
| `campaign_name`        | Campaign name containing area information |

`area_id` is derived from `campaign_name`.

Impressions are not represented as individual customer-level touchpoints.

---

### 13.6 `transactions`

**Grain:** one transaction / purchase.

| Column             | Purpose                                  |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| `transaction_id`   | Transaction identifier                   |
| `customer_id`      | Customer associated with the transaction |
| `transaction_date` | Purchase date                            |
| `revenue`          | Transaction revenue                      |
| `subsidy`          | Business-funded subsidy                  |
| `discount`         | Discount applied                         |
| `cancelled`        | Cancellation indicator                   |

Derived economics:

```text
Variable Cost = Subsidy + Discount

Contribution Margin = Revenue - Subsidy - Discount
```

## 16. Synthetic World Design

The synthetic data will represent one coherent OTA business and data-generating process.

The simulation is intended to contain realistic variation across areas, customers, channels, and time.

### Area Heterogeneity

Areas may differ in:

* Area size
* Baseline demand
* Purchase propensity
* Average order value
* Customer mix
* Marketing intensity
* Seasonality

`area_size` is treated as metadata and is not intended to drive the analytical models directly.

### Customer Heterogeneity

Customers may differ in:

* Purchase propensity
* Average order value
* Likelihood of repeat purchase
* Product or category preference
* Price sensitivity
* Other characteristics that may not be directly observable

### Channel Behavior

Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM will have different underlying response characteristics.

Marketing effects will be probabilistic rather than deterministic.

The synthetic world will include diminishing returns so that marginal-return analysis and budget optimization are meaningful.

The final relative performance of the channels will not be predetermined.

## 19. Planned Analytical Workstreams

These describe the planned workflow and are not completed work.

### Workstream 1 - Marketing Data Foundation

Establish a trustworthy analytical dataset through data-quality and measurement checks.

### Workstream 2 - Attribution

Evaluate reported marketing performance and implement multi-touch attribution using the 14-day primary attribution window.

### Workstream 3 - Incrementality

Use the geographic experiment to estimate aggregate causal marketing impact.

### Workstream 4 - Customer Economics

Evaluate customer value, repeat behavior, acquisition economics, and contribution margin.

### Workstream 5 - Marginal Returns

Estimate channel response curves, diminishing returns, and marginal contribution-margin economics.

### Workstream 6 - Budget Optimization

Determine how the additional $2M should be allocated across eligible channels.

The optimization objective is:

> Maximize expected incremental contribution margin subject to the available budget and relevant business constraints.

### Workstream 7 - Executive Decision

Translate the final analysis into a business-facing investment decision supported by evidence, uncertainty, assumptions, and limitations.
