# Project Context

## 1. Project Overview

**Project:** Marketing Incrementality & Budget Allocation
**Project Type:** Marketing Analytics / Incrementality / Budget Optimization
**Status:** In Development
**Data:** Synthetic, controlled simulation
**Primary Business Outcome:** Incremental Contribution Margin

This project develops an end-to-end analytical framework for evaluating marketing effectiveness and determining how an additional marketing budget should be allocated across channels.

The analysis is designed to move from raw marketing and customer data toward a financially grounded investment decision.

---

## 2. Business Question

The company currently spends approximately **$10M annually** across:

* Google
* Meta
* TikTok
* CRM
* Organic

The CMO has an additional **$2M** available for investment in the next quarter.

The central decision is:

> **Where should the additional $2M be invested, and how much incremental business should the company expect from that investment?**

The project evaluates this question through measurement, causal inference, customer economics, marginal return analysis, and budget optimization.

---

## 3. Analytical Objective

The primary objective is to estimate the **incremental contribution margin** generated by additional marketing investment and use that estimate to support budget allocation.

The analytical framework distinguishes between:

* Attributed performance and incremental impact
* Revenue and contribution margin
* Average returns and marginal returns
* First-purchase value and longer-term customer value
* Statistical significance and business significance
* Expected outcomes and uncertainty

## 5. Core Analytical Principles

### 5.1 Attribution ≠ Incrementality

Attributed conversions describe which channel receives credit under an attribution methodology.

Incrementality estimates the business that would not have occurred without the marketing activity.

Attribution is therefore treated as a measurement framework rather than direct evidence of causal impact.

### 5.2 Revenue ≠ Contribution Margin

Revenue alone does not represent the economic value generated by marketing.

Contribution margin is the primary financial outcome used for investment decisions.

### 5.3 Average Return ≠ Marginal Return

Historical average performance does not necessarily describe the return generated by additional spending.

The project therefore focuses on the economics of incremental investment.

### 5.4 First Purchase Value ≠ Customer Value

Acquisition economics are evaluated beyond the initial transaction.

Customer value is analyzed across multiple time horizons, including:

* 30 days
* 90 days
* 180 days
* 365 days

### 5.5 Statistical Significance ≠ Business Significance

Statistical evidence is evaluated alongside the magnitude and economic relevance of the observed effect.

### 5.6 Recommendation ≠ Certainty

The final allocation is presented together with assumptions, uncertainty, sensitivity, and limitations.

## 6. Scope of Work

The project consists of seven analytical workstreams.

### Workstream 1 — Marketing Data Foundation

Establish a trustworthy analytical dataset by addressing:

* Missing campaign and tracking identifiers
* Duplicate records
* Customer identity inconsistencies
* Date and timezone issues
* Attribution-window differences
* Platform and warehouse discrepancies
* Unmatched records
* Experiment contamination

### Workstream 2 — Attribution

Calculate reported channel performance including:

* Spend
* Impressions
* Clicks
* Visits
* Bookings
* First purchases
* Revenue
* Contribution margin
* CAC
* Conversion rate
* ROAS
* CM-ROAS

### Workstream 3 — Incrementality

Evaluate causal marketing impact using a geographic experiment consisting of:

* 20 markets
* 10 treatment markets
* 10 control markets
* Pre-experiment period
* 28-day experiment period

The analysis includes:

* Randomization diagnostics
* Treatment/control balance
* Pre-period trends
* Exposure
* Contamination
* Seasonality
* Difference-in-Differences estimation
* Statistical uncertainty
* Business significance

Primary incremental outcomes:

* Incremental customers
* Incremental conversion
* Incremental revenue
* Incremental contribution margin

### Workstream 4 — Customer Economics

Evaluate customer economics across acquisition channels and cohorts.

Key measures include:

* Revenue
* Contribution margin
* Number of purchases
* Repeat purchase rate
* Average order value
* Customer value / LTV

### Workstream 5 — Marginal Returns

Estimate how incremental marketing investment translates into incremental economic value.

The analysis examines:

* Response curves
* Diminishing returns
* Average return
* Marginal return
* Saturation
* Incremental contribution margin

### Workstream 6 — Budget Optimization

Determine an allocation of the additional **$2M** across channels.

The optimization objective is:

> Maximize expected incremental contribution margin subject to the available budget and relevant business constraints.

Potential constraints include:

* Channel spending limits
* Minimum or maximum investment levels
* CAC constraints
* Response-curve saturation
* Operational limitations

### Workstream 7 — Executive Decision

Translate the analytical results into an investment decision containing:

* Proposed $2M allocation
* Expected incremental contribution margin
* Expected range
* Supporting evidence
* Assumptions
* Key uncertainties
* Risks
* Recommended next experiment or measurement improvement

## 7. Data Strategy

The project uses **controlled synthetic data** designed to represent a realistic marketing measurement environment.

The data is generated from a coherent underlying data-generating process rather than from independent random datasets.

The simulation contains known ground truth for selected causal and economic relationships.

Ground truth is used primarily to validate whether analytical methods can recover known effects. It is not treated as the primary analytical input.

---

## 8. Analytical Funnel

The simulated customer journey follows:

```text
Impression
    ↓
Click
    ↓
Visit
    ↓
Booking
    ↓
First Purchase
    ↓
Repeat Purchase
    ↓
Contribution Margin
```

Core funnel metrics include:

* CTR = Clicks / Impressions
* Visit Rate = Visits / Clicks
* Booking Rate = Bookings / Visits
* First Purchase Conversion = First Purchases / Bookings
* Repeat Purchase Rate = Repeat Purchasers / First Purchasers

---

## 9. Key Financial Metrics

### ROAS

```text
ROAS = Attributed Revenue / Marketing Spend
```

### CAC

```text
CAC = Marketing Spend / Acquired Customers
```

### CM-ROAS

```text
CM-ROAS = Attributed Contribution Margin / Marketing Spend
```

The project additionally evaluates **incremental contribution margin** and **marginal contribution-margin return** because these measures are more directly connected to the investment decision.
