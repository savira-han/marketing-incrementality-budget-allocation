# Project Context

## 1. Project Overview

**Project:** Marketing Incrementality & Budget Allocation
**Project Type:** Marketing Analytics / Incrementality / Budget Optimization
**Status:** Day 1 - Business & Measurement Design complete
**Data:** Synthetic, controlled simulation
**Primary Business Outcome:** Incremental Contribution Margin

This project develops an end-to-end marketing analytics framework for deciding how an additional marketing budget should be allocated across channels.

The analysis is designed to move from marketing and customer data toward a financially grounded investment decision, while distinguishing reported performance from causal impact and average performance from marginal return.

At the current stage, the business and measurement design has been defined. Data generation and analysis have not yet started.

---

## 2. Business Question

The company currently spends approximately **$10M annually** across:

* Google
* Meta
* TikTok
* CRM
* Organic

The CMO has an additional **$2M** available for investment in the next quarter.

The central decision is:

> Given an additional $2M marketing budget for the next quarter, how should the budget be allocated across Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM to maximize expected incremental contribution margin?

The analysis will account for:

* Incremental impact
* Customer economics
* Diminishing returns
* Marginal return
* Operational constraints
* Uncertainty

The final allocation is **not known in advance**.

The primary objective is to estimate and optimize:

The project distinguishes between:

## 6. Core Analytical Principles

### 6.1 Attribution ≠ Incrementality

Attributed conversions describe which channel receives credit under an attribution methodology.

Incrementality asks what would have happened without the marketing activity.

Attribution is therefore treated as a descriptive measurement framework, not direct evidence of causal impact.

### 6.2 Revenue ≠ Contribution Margin

Revenue alone does not represent the economic value generated by marketing.

Contribution margin is the primary financial outcome used for the investment decision.

### 6.3 Average Return ≠ Marginal Return

Historical average performance does not necessarily describe the return from additional spending.

The project therefore focuses on the economics of incremental investment.

### 6.4 First Purchase Value ≠ Customer Value

Customer economics may extend beyond the initial transaction.

First purchase and repeat-purchase behavior will be used where relevant for customer economics and customer value analysis.

However, standard campaign performance will use **Purchase** as the campaign conversion outcome rather than routinely splitting performance into first and repeat purchases.

### 6.5 Statistical Significance ≠ Business Significance

Statistical evidence will be evaluated alongside effect size and economic relevance.

### 6.6 Recommendation ≠ Certainty

The final allocation will be presented with assumptions, uncertainty, sensitivity, and limitations.

## 8. Causal Measurement Design

The project will use a **geographic controlled experiment** to establish aggregate causal evidence.

### Experiment Design

* 20 markets
* 10 treatment markets
* 10 control markets
* 28-day experiment
* Treatment receives a 25% increase in total marketing spend
* Control remains at baseline spend
* Treatment and control are assigned using stratified randomization
* Stratification is based on pre-experiment market characteristics
* Baseline balance and pre-period trends will be checked before estimating the treatment effect

A **market** is defined as a geographic business unit with independently observable marketing activity and customer outcomes.

Markets do not need to be identical. The design instead aims to create credible comparability through stratified randomization and subsequent diagnostics.

Potential characteristics for stratification and balance checks include:

* Baseline purchases
* Baseline revenue
* Baseline marketing spend
* Market size
* Historical purchase behavior
* Average order value
* Customer mix

The exact market variables have not yet been finalized.

## 9. Difference-in-Differences

The primary causal estimator will be **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)**.

Conceptually:

```text
Incremental Effect
=
Change in Treatment
-
Change in Control
```

The primary causal outcome is **contribution margin**.

Supporting outcomes include:

* Purchases
* Revenue

The key identifying assumption is that treatment and control markets would have followed sufficiently similar trends in the absence of the treatment.

Diagnostics will therefore include:

* Baseline balance
* Pre-treatment trends
* Treatment exposure
* Contamination
* Seasonality
* Market shocks
* Statistical uncertainty

---

## 10. Aggregate Experiment vs Channel-Level Effects

The geographic experiment estimates the **aggregate incremental effect of increasing total marketing investment**.

It does not automatically identify the individual causal effect of:

* Google
* Meta
* TikTok
* CRM

All four channels are increased together in the treatment condition.

The planned treatment structure preserves the existing 4:3:2:1 channel mix while increasing total marketing spend by 25%.

For example, a hypothetical $100K baseline would become $125K:

| Channel   |  Baseline | Treatment |
| --------- | --------: | --------: |
| Google    |      $40K |      $50K |
| Meta      |      $30K |    $37.5K |
| TikTok    |      $20K |      $25K |
| CRM       |      $10K |    $12.5K |
| **Total** | **$100K** | **$125K** |

This means:

> **Experiment = aggregate causal evidence**

while:

> **Channel response models + attribution + experiment evidence = channel-level marginal economics**

The aggregate experiment effect should not be attributed to an individual channel without sufficient identification.

## 11. Synthetic World Design

The synthetic data will represent one coherent business and data-generating process rather than a collection of independent random datasets.

The planned historical period is approximately **18 months**, followed by the experiment period and an observation window for downstream customer behavior.

The exact dates have not yet been defined.

### Market Heterogeneity

Markets may differ in:

* Market size
* Baseline demand
* Purchase propensity
* Average order value
* Customer mix
* Marketing intensity
* Seasonality

### Customer Heterogeneity

Customers may differ in:

* Purchase propensity
* Average order value
* Likelihood of repeat purchase
* Product or category preference
* Price sensitivity
* Other characteristics that may not be directly observable

### Channel Behavior

Google, Meta, TikTok, and CRM will have different underlying response characteristics.

Marketing effects will be probabilistic rather than deterministic.

The synthetic world will include **diminishing returns** so that marginal-return analysis and budget optimization are meaningful.

The final relative performance of the channels will not be predetermined.

## 12. Hidden Simulation Truth

The simulation will contain hidden ground-truth artifacts under:

```text
data/simulation_truth/
```

Planned files:

```text
true_channel_incrementality.csv
true_customer_value.csv
true_market_effect.csv
true_response_curves.csv
```

These represent known underlying relationships within the synthetic data-generating process.

They are intended for **validation**, not as primary analytical inputs.

The purpose is to evaluate whether the analytical methods can recover the underlying relationships with reasonable accuracy.

The estimates are not expected to match the ground truth exactly because the synthetic world will include noise and realistic variation.
